## The Fragment

A short Sanskrit manuscript fragment — beginning mid-word with `r vvināśaḥ` — came to us without identification. The text is dense Pramāṇa-school (*Dignāga/Dharmakīrti* tradition) philosophical prose, working through three interlocking epistemological problems:

1. Whether *anupalabdhi* (non-apprehension) of a pot at the sherd-moment (*kapālakāla*) establishes its non-existence, and what *dṛśyatā* (perceptibility) consists in
2. Whether re-identification (*punarupalambha*) of a perceived object across temporal moments is possible via *pratyakṣa* or *anumāna*
3. The privacy of perceptual streams across mental continua (*santānāntara*)

The text is unmistakably a *bhāṣya* (commentary prose), sustaining a *pūrvapakṣa*–*uttarapakṣa* dialectic throughout.

## Identifying the Text

Using the Dharmamitra corpus search tools together with Claude, we ran the most distinctive compound terms against the parallel passage database. Two phrases stood out immediately:

- **`avikalasmṛtisaṃskāra`** ("seed of non-confused memory") — a diagnostic compound found in the Tibetan of D4230 at folio 12b–13b
- **`sarvasādhakapratyakṣa`** ("perception of all proponents") — confirmed in D4230 at folio 13a–b

Both terms pointed to the Tibetan Tengyur. The argument sequence — *dṛśyānupalabdhi* → *kapālakāla* → *santānāntara* → *punarupalambha/pūrvatā* — matched folios 10b–13b of D4230 exactly, in a single continuous passage.

**D4230** is **Vinītadeva's *Nyāyabinduprakaraṇa*** (*Śiṣyahita*), translated by Jinamitra and Yeshé Dé. The colophon at folio 36b-2 names the author explicitly: *sloB dpon dul ba'i lhas mdzad pa* ("composed by Ācārya Vinītadeva").

### A Sanskrit Confirmation

Pārthasārathi Miśra's *Śāstradīpikā* (SA_GSP28_sastrdiu:3143) quotes the *darśanayogyatā* definition verbatim:

> *dṛśyatā darśanayogyatā tasyāmavasthāyāṃ nāstīti tena rūpeṇābhāvāt dṛśyaṃ nāstīty ucyate*

This matches the D4230 text precisely. Pārthasārathi does not name his source, but given the identification of the fragment as Vinītadeva's *Nyāyabinduprakaraṇa*, his quotation is consistent with that text — and confirms that the *darśanayogyatā* definition was circulating in Sanskrit.

## The Reconstruction

The fragment has eight lacunae. We reconstructed them by back-translating the Tibetan of D4230 and matching surrounding syntax. Confidence varies:

| Lacuna | Confidence | Basis |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| L1 | ◆ Attested | D4230:10b-23–25 + Pārthasārathi verbatim |
| L2 | ◇ Probable | D4230:13a-2–13b-2 |
| L3 | ◇ Probable | D4230:13a-9–13a-17 (transition zone) |
| L4 | ◆ Attested | D4230:12b-13–13a-5 (`avikalasmṛtisaṃskāra`) |
| L5 | ◇ Probable | D4230:13a-24–27 |
| L6 | ○ Speculative | No direct Tibetan segment; argument context only |
| L7 | ◇ Probable | D4230:13b-4–9 |
| L8 | ◆ Attested | D4230:13b-9–24 (`ekādaśadhā`) |

◆ Attested (back-translation from Tibetan D4230) · ◇ Probable · ○ Speculative

The text runs from the *dṛśyatā* definition through to the introduction of Dharmakīrti's eleven-fold *anupalabdhi* typology — the folio ending precisely at `ekādaśadhā`, just before the enumeration begins.

## What the Fragment Establishes

The Sanskrit of this section of Vinītadeva's *Nyāyabinduprakaraṇa* is unedited. No Sanskrit parallels for the most distinctive phrases (`svaparasambhavī grāhyākāre bhedako`, `paścāddarśanānumeyāpi sthāyitā`) appear anywhere in the DharmaMitra corpus — consistent with an unedited text preserved only in Tibetan translation. This fragment is, to our knowledge, the first direct Sanskrit witness to this passage.

The philological upshot: the Dharmakīrti commentary tradition in Sanskrit is less thoroughly lost than the absence of editions implies. The Tibetan translations are precise enough to guide confident reconstruction when a fragment surfaces.
