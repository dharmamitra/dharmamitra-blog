On November 4, we welcomed a delegation from Fo Guang Shan to Sendai, where the Fo Guang Shan Institute of Humanistic Buddhism and the Center for Integrated Japanese Studies at Tohoku University signed a Memorandum of Understanding. The agreement continues the collaboration between MITRA and FoGuangAI on AI translation and advanced search of Buddhist texts.

Our work together began already in 2024 at UC Berkeley, where we signed the first MoU to support the development of AI applications for the translation of Fo Guang Shan related material. By combining MITRA’s multilingual Buddhist dataset with Fo Guang Shan’s bilingual corpus consisting of Buddhist texts and Humanistic Buddhism, we jointly developed the FoGuangAI translation platform. With MITRA now at Tohoku University, the new agreement renews ans strengthens this commitment. 

The signing took place at the Graduate School of Arts and Letters on the Kawauchi South Campus. The Fo Guang Shan delegation of seven was led by Venerable Miao Guang, Deputy Director of the Institute and Editor-in-Chief of the English edition of the Fo Guang Dictionary of Buddhism, together with Venerable Man Run, Head Abbess of Fo Guang Shan Japan. Tohoku University was represented by nine members, including Center Director Prof. Hiroaki Adachi, Deputy Director Prof. Satoshi Kato, Prof. Christopher Craig, Assoc. Prof. Ryuta Kikuya, and assistant professor Sebastian Nehrdich of the MITRA team.

In his remarks, Prof. Adachi welcomed the partnership and expressed his hope that it will open new opportunities for Buddhist studies and international academic exchange. Venerable Miao Guang thanked the university for its support and spoke of Fo Guang Shan’s long commitment to preserving and translating Buddhist literature, following the vision of its founder, Venerable Master Hsing Yun. She expressed the hope that responsible use of AI can help lay a foundation for Buddhist scholarship and cultural transmission in the decades ahead.

The next phase will focus on developing and refining FoGuangAI. We plan to draw on the newly published English edition of the Fo Guang Dictionary of Buddhism to improve the model’s handling of Buddhist terminology, textual context, and doctrinal meaning, with the goal of more accurate and consistent translations that support the work of Buddhist translators.

After the ceremony, the delegation visited the Tohoku University Library to view its Derge edition of the Tibetan Buddhist canon, as well as the University Archives.
We are grateful to Fo Guang Shan for their trust and partnership, and we look forward to continuing this work together from Sendai!
